The pricing model

Quote-only. No public pricing published. Free mPulse Lite trial (30 days, 1M beacons/mo) available for evaluation.

Why mPulse is quote-only

Akamai sells mPulse primarily into Akamai's existing CDN and edge-platform customer base. The mPulse product is rarely sold standalone, it tends to be bundled with broader Akamai enterprise contracts that include CDN, security (App & API Protector, Bot Manager), edge compute, and image/video management. Bundle economics make standalone per-product pricing irrelevant to most actual mPulse purchases.

For procurement teams not already on Akamai, the mPulse sales conversation typically starts with the Akamai account team or a partner, includes a discovery on traffic volume and feature requirements, runs through the Lite trial for proof-of-concept, and ends in a custom enterprise quote anchored to total Akamai annual spend. We have not seen a published per-beacon rate or even a tier structure on the Akamai pricing page.

The mPulse Lite trial

Akamai publishes one piece of concrete pricing: the mPulse Lite product. It's a 30-day free trial covering up to 1 million beacons per month. A “beacon” in mPulse terminology is roughly equivalent to a page-view event with associated performance and timing data. 1M beacons covers a small commercial site for the trial period. After 30 days, the trial expires; continued use requires a paid mPulse contract negotiated through Akamai.

What drives an mPulse quote

Because there is no published rate, the only honest thing to say about an mPulse price is what moves it. A quote is built from your monthly beacon volume (mPulse meters page-view-equivalent beacons), the feature set (waterfall, third-party impact analysis, synthetic checks), and, above all, whether mPulse rides an existing Akamai contract or is bought standalone. Akamai anchors mPulse to total account value, so the same beacon volume can quote very differently for an existing CDN customer versus a net-new buyer.

We deliberately do not publish an inferred dollar figure. Akamai discloses no per-beacon rate, no tier structure, and no list price, and any specific number quoted without an Akamai proposal in hand is a guess. How mPulse compares

  • Versus Datadog RUM, Datadog publishes roughly $3.15/1K sessions for RUM (Measure + Investigate, annual). mPulse does not publish a comparable rate, so with no mPulse figure to anchor against, the two cannot be compared directly on price.
  • Versus Catchpoint, both compete in the enterprise quote-only RUM + synthetic space. Catchpoint publishes a $10K/year floor (Quick Start) which is at least a published anchor; mPulse offers no equivalent.
  • Versus Cloudflare, Cloudflare Web Analytics is free and uncapped but offers much less feature breadth than mPulse (no advanced waterfall, no third-party impact analysis, no synthetic checks).
  • Versus SpeedCurve, SpeedCurve publishes from $90/month with similar RUM + synthetic bundled product positioning, sitting at the opposite end of the pricing-transparency spectrum from mPulse.

The operational cost of quote-only

See our quote-only vendors deep-dive for the full operational consequences. Briefly: a typical mPulse procurement takes 4-6 weeks from initial contact to signed contract; requires an NDA before benchmarked pricing is disclosed; can rarely be benchmarked against published competitor rates in the same procurement cycle; and creates “list price never seen” lock-in that makes future re-negotiation difficult.

When mPulse is the right choice

  • You are already on Akamai for CDN, security, or edge compute and want one combined contract.
  • You are a large enterprise with traffic volume that makes standalone RUM vendor pricing infeasible.
  • You need integration with Akamai's edge platform features (RUM-informed routing, image optimisation triggers, etc.).

When mPulse is the wrong choice

  • You are not on Akamai for any other product, the bundled-economics rationale disappears.
  • You need transparent published pricing for procurement compliance.
  • Your traffic volume is mid-market scale, every published-pricing alternative is more cost-predictable.
